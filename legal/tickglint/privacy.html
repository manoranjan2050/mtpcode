Tickglint Privacy Policy

Tickglint: Smart Clock · Package com.mtpcode.tickglint · Effective 9 October 2026

This policy is for the Android app Tickglint: Smart Clock (com.mtpcode.tickglint), published by MTP Code / Manoranjan (ElectroIoT). Contact: admin@mtpcode.com.

Tickglint sets up and controls the Tickglint smart clock (an ESP32 board with a 12864 LCD) that you build and own. The app has no account, no ads, no analytics and no tracking, and it does not sell or share personal data.

What data the app uses

  • Clock settings (alarms, time zone, brightness, clock face, special days, messages, animations and similar) are read from and written to your own clock over Bluetooth or your local Wi-Fi. They are stored on the clock, not on a server.
  • Time: the app sends your phone's current time to the clock so it can show the right time without Wi-Fi.
  • City / location for sunrise and sunset: you choose a city from a list or type coordinates; they are sent to and stored on your clock. The app never reads your phone's location.
  • Wi-Fi network name and password, only when you set up the clock's Wi-Fi. The app scans networks through the clock and sends the password to the clock over the encrypted Bluetooth link; the app does not keep it.
  • Messages you send to the clock are sent to it and the last few are remembered on your phone to reuse them.
  • Bluetooth pairing: Android stores the pairing with your clock. Pairing uses LE Secure Connections with a 6-digit PIN shown on the clock's display.
  • Firmware files: when you update or install clock firmware, the app downloads firmware files and keeps them temporarily in its cache on your phone.
  • Diagnostics, crash reports, contacts, photos, accounts, location history: not collected.

Permissions and why

  • Bluetooth scan / connect (Nearby devices): to find Tickglint clocks near you and talk to them. It is declared never used for location.
  • Location (Android 8 to 11 only): Android requires it for any Bluetooth scan on those versions. Tickglint never reads or stores your location.
  • Internet and network state: to find a clock on your Wi-Fi, to read the firmware list and the setup-guide links, to download firmware you ask for, and to open links you tap.
  • USB host (optional): only when you use Install firmware, to write firmware to a board you plug into the phone.

Network requests

The app downloads, without sending any personal data: the firmware list and firmware files and the setup-guide link file from mtpcode.com. Requests are made over HTTPS and only the usual connection details (such as your IP address) are visible to the web server.

Your clock

The Tickglint clock itself can connect to your Wi-Fi to get the internet time from public NTP time servers. It has a settings web page on your local network; you can protect it with an access key in the app.

Children

The app is not directed at children and does not knowingly collect personal information from anyone.

Your choices

  • Deny Bluetooth or USB permission in Android settings: the app still opens, features that need them will not work.
  • Remove the pairing with a clock in Android Bluetooth settings, or use the clock's "forget paired phones" action.
  • Uninstall the app, or clear its storage, to delete everything it keeps on your phone.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version is published on this page with a new effective date.
